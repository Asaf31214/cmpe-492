# Clinical Evidence: Preoperative Predictors of Pediatric PCNL Success

## Stone Burden
- Stone burden is the **strongest and most consistent predictor** of stone-free status.
- Mean burden: stone-free 257±415 mm² vs residual 375±256 mm² (p<0.001, n=580).
- PCNL preferred when stone burden >2 cm². For stones >15 mm, PCNL achieves 94% clearance vs 55% for URS.
- Staghorn calculi (>4 cm, all calyces): OR 2.34 for partial clearance.
- Stone volume, density (HU), and multiplicity are also significant (p<0.001, p=0.019, p=0.001).

## Number of Stones / Calyces Involved
- Multiple stones independently reduce SFR (p=0.001).
- Number of calyces involved: OR 4.02 (95% CI 1.11–14.53, p=0.033) for residual fragments.

## Guy's Stone Score (GSS)
- Only independent predictor on multivariate analysis in multiple pediatric studies.
- Pediatric SFR by grade (Kırlı et al. 2020, n=197): Grade 1: 89%, Grade 2: 78%, Grade 3: 75%, Grade 4: 57%. Overall: 77%.
- GSS Grade 4: 4.17% of stone-free vs 24.21% of residual (p<0.001, n=580).
- Multivariate OR 3.14 (95% CI 1.31–7.49, p=0.01) per grade increase.
- Correlates with residual status: r=+0.251 (p<0.001).

## Anatomical Anomalies
- Structural anomalies (horseshoe, ectopic, malrotated, duplicated system, bifid pelvis) modestly reduce SFR.
- In a large series (n=580): 0/29 stone-free patients had anomalies vs 6/8 residual patients with anomalies (p=0.009).
- Likely mechanism: abnormal pelvicalyceal orientation, suboptimal puncture angles, poorer drainage.
- Horseshoe kidney: initial SFR 72% in children vs 87% in adults; after auxiliary procedures both ~93–94%.

## Patient Age
- Median age: stone-free 8 years vs residual 6 years (p=0.011, n=580).
- Age 11–17: 48.9% of stone-free vs 38.9% of residual.
- Older children likely benefit from larger renal anatomy → better instrumentation access.
- However, age is NOT an independent predictor when stone burden is controlled.

## Positive Urine Culture
- Does NOT independently predict stone-free status (p=0.085 in n=580).
- Strong predictor of infectious complications: ~4x higher sepsis risk (8.4% vs 2.2%, p=0.004).
- E. coli cultures carry 43.9% post-op infection rate, 60% progress to septic shock.

## Prior Stone Surgery
- Does NOT independently predict worse SFR (p=0.079–0.886 across studies).
- May create technical difficulty (perirenal fibrosis) but success rates comparable.

## Preoperative Creatinine / GFR
- Does NOT predict stone-free status (p>0.05 in multiple series).
- PCNL can improve renal function when obstruction is relieved.
- Elevated creatinine in revision cases reflects disease complexity, not surgical difficulty per se.
